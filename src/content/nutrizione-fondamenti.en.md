## The scientific method applied to diet

Before talking about FODMAPs, you need to understand *why* a diet works. The literature is blunt on one point: **the only feature common to all effective weight-loss diets is an energy deficit**. Low-carb, low-fat, ketogenic, Mediterranean — in controlled trials at equal adherence, weight loss tracks energy balance, not macronutrient composition (Hall & Guo, 2017; Gardner et al., DIETFITS 2018).

This does not mean "calories are everything": energy balance is the *physical constraint*, while **diet composition determines satiety, adherence, metabolic health and — in our case — gut symptoms**.

## The three macronutrients in brief

- **Protein**: 4 kcal/g. Structural components (muscle, enzymes, epithelial barrier). The target for preserving lean mass is 1.6-2.2 g/kg (ISSN, Jäger 2017). High satiety, high thermic effect (~25%).
- **Carbohydrates**: 4 kcal/g. Rapid energy. For IBS the critical distinction is not "good/bad" but **absorbable vs fermentable**: carbohydrates escaping digestion become bacterial substrate in the colon.
- **Fats**: 9 kcal/g. Required for cell membranes, steroid hormones, absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K). A concentrated load triggers the gastrocolic reflex via cholecystokinin (Feinle-Bisset 2013).

## Micronutrients that matter

Beyond macronutrients, the gut and metabolism depend on vitamins and minerals:

- **Magnesium**: cofactor in >300 enzymatic reactions; deficit correlates with cramps and constipation. Sources: pumpkin seeds, almonds, spinach, quinoa.
- **Iron**: heme (meat, fish) more bioavailable than non-heme (plants); vitamin C aids absorption. Iron-deficiency anemia is a diagnostic *red flag*.
- **Calcium + Vitamin D**: vitamin D is more hormone than vitamin; deficiency is common in dairy-avoiders. Low-FODMAP sources: oily fish, eggs, aged cheeses.
- **Zinc, Folate, B12**: epithelial repair and cell synthesis. Poorly managed elimination diets risk deficiencies — which is why phase 1 is capped at 2-6 weeks.
- **Iodine, Selenium**: thyroid function — underrated in the differential diagnosis of GI symptoms.

The calculator sets protein, fat, carbohydrate and fiber targets from biometric data; the food database shows kcal, macros and micronutrients per 100 g. The three-phase diet manages *tolerance*, not weight loss: if your goal is losing weight, the mechanism remains an energy deficit, but you must reach it without worsening IBS symptoms — and that is exactly the problem this app solves.
