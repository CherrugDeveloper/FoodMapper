## Why sleep is a metabolic variable

Sleep is not simply a pause. It is an active state in which the autonomic nervous system, insulin sensitivity, blood pressure and signals that influence hunger are regulated. Duration, quality and regularity matter together. One short night does not "break" metabolism, but repeated restriction or fragmented sleep can gradually shift several risk factors.

## Short sleep, glucose and insulin

Experimental studies show that a few days of reduced sleep can worsen insulin sensitivity and glucose tolerance, even in young, healthy adults. The result depends on duration, timing, physical activity, diet and individual vulnerability. Shift work and circadian misalignment may add an effect because meals and light exposure occur during a biologically less favorable phase.

This does not mean that one short night causes diabetes. Type 2 diabetes is multifactorial and requires clinical assessment; sleep is one modifiable part of the overall picture.

## Hunger, ghrelin and eating behavior

Sleep loss has been associated in some studies with more hunger, stronger cravings for highly rewarding foods and more opportunities to eat. Controlled experiments have found changes in ghrelin and leptin, but effects are not identical in everyone and cannot predict one person's behavior. Fatigue, stress and lower physical activity may also explain part of the association.

In a weight-loss trial, insufficient sleep reduced the proportion of weight lost as fat compared with adequate sleep, despite the same calorie restriction. This is informative, but it does not prove that increasing sleep alone causes weight loss.

## Practical guidance

For most adults, the general reference is about **7-9 hours of regular sleep**, with individual variation. It may help to:

- keep wake-up times reasonably consistent;
- get daylight exposure and be physically active during waking hours;
- limit afternoon caffeine and alcohol close to bedtime;
- keep the bedroom dark, quiet and cool;
- avoid using sleep advice as a substitute for prescribed treatment or a balanced diet.

If time in bed is adequate but sleep remains unrefreshing, or there is loud snoring, observed breathing pauses, gasping awakenings or daytime sleepiness, discuss it with a professional. Obstructive sleep apnoea may require specific assessment.

## Interpretation and limits

Associations between sleep and metabolism may be influenced by obesity, depression, medicines, shift work and socioeconomic conditions. No number of hours diagnoses or excludes a disease. A diary of sleep timing, awakenings, caffeine, activity and symptoms can describe context, but it does not replace a consultation or prescribed tests.
