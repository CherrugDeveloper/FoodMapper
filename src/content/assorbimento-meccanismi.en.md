## The three mechanisms that turn food into symptoms

IBS is not "an intolerance": it is a physicochemical cascade. Three documented mechanisms convert unabsorbed molecules into pain, bloating and transit changes.

## 1. The osmotic effect — water recruitment

Small hydrophilic molecules that escape absorption (polyols such as sorbitol and mannitol, undigested lactose, excess fructose) remain in the lumen and **pull water into the intestine** down the osmotic gradient. Murray et al. (2014) measured this by MRI: FODMAPs significantly increase small-bowel water content.

Consequences: diluted chyme → accelerated transit → loose stools and urgency; the water surplus in the colon contributes to diarrhea. This is why a "harmless" fruit can trigger symptoms in 30-90 minutes: speed is the signature of osmosis, not fermentation.

## 2. Bacterial fermentation — the gas engine

Oligosaccharides (fructans from wheat, garlic, onion; galactans from legumes) have no dedicated human enzymes: they reach the colon intact, where the microbiota ferments them **within hours**, producing H₂, CH₄ and CO₂. Methane specifically slows transit — which is why the IBS-C subtype often shows methane-dominant breath-test profiles (Pimentel et al.).

Key point: fermentation is *physiological* (the microbiota feeds us SCFAs). The IBS problem is not gas production per se but its **amplified perception** — see mechanism 3.

## 3. Luminal distension — when pressure becomes pain

Water (osmosis) + gas (fermentation) = volume stretching the colonic wall. In healthy subjects mechanoreceptors register a neutral event; in IBS, **visceral hypersensitivity** — a lowered threshold of stretch receptors plus central amplification along the gut-brain axis — turns the same distension into sharp pain, cramps and debilitating bloating (Deiteren 2016).

This is why the low-FODMAP diet works: it does not "cure" hypersensitivity, but **reduces the volume that triggers it**.

## Summary

| Mechanism | Key molecule | Dominant symptom | Typical timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Osmosis | Polyols, lactose, fructose | Diarrhea, urgency | 30-90 min |
| Fermentation | Fructans, galactans | Bloating, gas, pain | 4-12 h |
| Distension | Consequence of 1+2 | Pain, cramps | Ongoing |
