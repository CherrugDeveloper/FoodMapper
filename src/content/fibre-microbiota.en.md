## Soluble vs insoluble fiber: two opposite mechanisms

"Fiber" is not one thing. The clinically relevant split is **water solubility**, because it completely changes luminal behavior:

- **Soluble fiber** (pectins, oat beta-glucans, psyllium, inulin): forms viscous gels, slows gastric emptying, is highly fermentable and yields **short-chain fatty acids** (SCFA: butyrate, propionate, acetate). Butyrate is the main fuel of colonocytes and modulates mucosal inflammation.
- **Insoluble fiber** (cellulose, lignins, wheat bran): does not dissolve, bulks the stool and mechanically speeds up transit. Useful in constipation, irritating in viscerally hypersensitive subjects.

## The microbiota as a metabolic organ

The gut microbiota (~10¹³ bacteria, ~150× the human genome in genes) ferments undigested residues and produces active metabolites:

1. **SCFA → energy and barrier**: butyrate feeds colonocytes and strengthens tight junctions (the "barrier" separating the lumen from immune tissue).
2. **Gas → distension**: H₂, CH₄, CO₂ are harmless *by quantity*, but problematic when mechanoreceptors are hypersensitive (see the IBS pathophysiology article).
3. **Eubiosis vs dysbiosis**: prolonged elimination diets reduce microbial diversity — a documented effect motivating phase-2 reintroduction.

## Psyllium: the fiber with the strongest IBS evidence

In Bijkerk et al.'s randomized trial (2009, BMJ), **psyllium** (soluble fiber) significantly reduced symptom severity versus bran and placebo. Practical note: psyllium is naturally low-FODMAP and increases fecal mass *without* explosive fermentation — which is why the calculator recommends predominantly soluble fiber in the IBS-D subtype and a gradual mix in IBS-C.

## Operating rules

- Gradual increments: +5 g/week, never abrupt jumps.
- Fiber without water = a plug: the hydration target (35 ml/kg) is part of the mechanism.
- In phase 1, fiber comes from low-FODMAP sources: oats, quinoa, chia seeds, carrots, oranges.
