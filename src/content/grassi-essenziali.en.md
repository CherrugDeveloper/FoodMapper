## Why fats cannot be eliminated

Lipids are not just energy (9 kcal/g): they are structural material. Every cell membrane is a phospholipid bilayer; steroid hormones (cortisol, testosterone, estrogens) derive from cholesterol; vitamins A, D, E, K absorb only in the presence of fat. A fat-free diet is physiologically impossible: the debate is about *which* fats and *how much* concentration per meal.

## Essential fatty acids

"Essential" = the body cannot synthesize them:

- **Omega-3 (ALA → EPA/DHA)**: precursors of anti-inflammatory mediators (resolvins, protectins). Strongest evidence: triglyceride reduction, inflammatory modulation. Low-FODMAP sources: oily fish (mackerel, sardines — richest in EPA/DHA), chia seeds, walnuts, flax oil.
- **Omega-6 (LA → arachidonic acid)**: essential but pro-inflammatory in relative excess. Ubiquitous in seed oils and industrial products: the modern problem is not deficiency but the unbalanced omega-6/omega-3 ratio.

## Fats and the gut: the gastrocolic reflex

The mechanism that matters for IBS: a concentrated lipid load makes the duodenum release **cholecystokinin (CCK)** and neurotensin. These hormones trigger the gastrocolic reflex — the motor wave pushing colonic content. In healthy subjects it produces the post-meal urge to use the bathroom; in IBS-D it produces spasms and urgency (Feinle-Bisset & Azpiroz 2013).

Practical implication: the problem is not fats but **spikes**. 10 g of EVO oil spread over 4 meals ≠ 40 g in one plate. The app's diet distributes the fat target across meals for exactly this reason.

## Operating rules

- **EVO oil** as the primary source: monounsaturates + anti-inflammatory polyphenols, naturally FODMAP-free.
- **Oily fish 2×/week**: EPA/DHA + vitamin D + iodine in a single low-FODMAP food.
- **Nuts and seeds in portions**: almonds (~10), walnuts, pumpkin and chia seeds — omegas, magnesium, zinc. Beyond portion they become an osmotic load.
- **Avoid spikes**: deep-fried foods and heavy sauces in a single meal are the classic IBS-D trigger.
