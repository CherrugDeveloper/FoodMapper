## Minimum caloric threshold in women: why 1200 kcal is not an arbitrary number

Many commercial apps and diets propose aggressive calorie cuts. However, physiological literature establishes a **metabolic safety floor** for adult women around 1200 kcal/day: below this threshold it becomes difficult to cover essential needs for protein, essential fatty acids, vitamins and minerals, even with an optimized diet.

## What the body risks below the threshold

1. **Metabolic adaptation**: resting energy expenditure (RMR) decreases defensively, making the deficit less effective over time (Rosenbaum & Leibel, 2010).
2. **Loss of lean mass**: without adequate protein intake and stimulus, muscle catabolism exceeds fat oxidation (Weinheimer et al., 2010).
3. **Hormonal alterations**: hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis, thyroid and HPA axis are affected by insufficient energy; in women this often manifests as hypothalamic amenorrhea (Loucks et al., 2011).
4. **Micronutrient deficits**: iron, calcium, iodine, folate, zinc and vitamin D are the most at-risk in women of childbearing age.
5. **Worsening of IBS symptoms**: very low calorie diets reduce food volume, may lower fiber intake and favor intestinal stasis.

## When the calculator applies the limit

The app uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation to estimate basal needs and multiplies it by the activity factor. If the result drops below 1200 kcal/day for a woman, the system **caps the value at 1200 kcal** and shows an explicit warning, while still maintaining structural protein and fat targets.

## The limit is not the same for everyone

- Athletes or very active women may need 1800-2400+ kcal/day even while in deficit.
- Postmenopausal, sedentary or petite women may be close to the minimum threshold without metabolic penalties.
- Pregnancy, lactation, convalescence and acute illness shift the limit upward.
