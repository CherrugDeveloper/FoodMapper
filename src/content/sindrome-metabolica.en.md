## What it is

**Metabolic syndrome** describes the coexistence of several cardiometabolic risk factors: central adiposity, high triglycerides, low HDL cholesterol, elevated blood pressure and elevated fasting glucose. It is not one disease or an inevitable fate; it signals a risk profile that merits clinical assessment and appropriate lifestyle interventions.

## Harmonized criteria

According to the IDF/AHA/NHLBI consensus, the research definition generally requires **at least 3 of 5** components:

1. elevated waist circumference according to ethnicity-specific and protocol-specific cut-offs;
2. triglycerides >=150 mg/dL (1.7 mmol/L) or treatment for hypertriglyceridemia;
3. HDL <40 mg/dL (1.0 mmol/L) in men or <50 mg/dL (1.3 mmol/L) in women, or relevant treatment;
4. blood pressure >=130/85 mmHg or antihypertensive treatment;
5. fasting glucose >=100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L) or treatment for hyperglycemia.

Criteria can differ between guidelines and populations. One reading is not always enough: blood pressure and laboratory tests require proper conditions, repeat testing when indicated and professional interpretation. Do not stop medicines to "fit" a range.

## Why it matters

The combination of components is associated with a higher risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease than having no such profile. Individual risk also depends on age, family history, smoking, kidney disease, medicines, sleep and inflammatory conditions. The syndrome does not precisely identify which mechanism causes risk and does not replace an overall cardiovascular-risk assessment.

## Prevention and lifestyle

Realistic, sustainable measures may include:

- a diet rich in vegetables, tolerated legumes, fruit, whole grains, fish and unsaturated fats, adapted to culture, budget and gut symptoms;
- reducing sugar-sweetened drinks, excess alcohol, trans fats and frequent ultra-processed foods;
- aerobic and resistance activity according to ability and medical conditions;
- regular sleep and assessment of snoring or sleepiness suggestive of apnoea;
- stopping smoking and managing stress;
- modest weight loss when appropriate, without extreme diets.

There is no need to wait for a diagnosis to improve these behaviors. When IBS or another condition is present, foods should be individualized: a restrictive diet is not automatically healthier.

## Monitoring and treatment

A doctor and dietitian can assess blood pressure, glucose/HbA1c, lipids, kidney function, medicines and overall risk. Some people need medication for blood pressure, lipids or glucose in addition to lifestyle measures. Teas, supplements and "detox" products do not cure metabolic syndrome and must not replace prescribed treatment.

## Disclaimer and limits

This article is informational, not diagnostic. Cut-offs and interpretation should be contextualized by a professional, especially in pregnancy, adolescence, older age, kidney or liver disease, medicine use or symptoms. Chest pain, sudden breathing difficulty or acute neurological signs require urgent care.
