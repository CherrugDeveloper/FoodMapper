## The Mifflin-St Jeor equation: how it estimates basal metabolism

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is today the most widely used method to estimate **Resting Metabolic Rate (RMR)** in healthy and overweight adults. Published in 1990, it surpassed previous formulas such as Harris-Benedict and Owen in accuracy for the modern population, which has a different body composition than historical samples.

## The formula

For women: **RMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age in years) − 161**

For men: **RMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age in years) + 5**

## What the terms mean

- **Weight**: total body mass is the dominant predictor of resting energy expenditure.
- **Height**: used to estimate body surface area and therefore heat exchange.
- **Age**: after age 30, RMR progressively decreases, mainly due to loss of lean mass.
- **Sex constants**: reflect the average difference in lean mass and fat between men and women.

## Limits and margins of error

The equation predicts RMR with an average error of ~10% in the general population. It is less accurate in:
- Athletes with very high muscle mass or very low body fat.
- Subjects with severe obesity (class III).
- People with thyroid disorders, fever or acute metabolic stress states.

## From estimate to real requirement

To obtain **Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)**, RMR is multiplied by an activity factor:

- Sedentary: × 1.2
- Lightly active: × 1.375
- Moderately active: × 1.55
- Very active: × 1.725

The application calculates TDEE and, if necessary, applies **safety minimum limits** (1200 kcal for women, 1500 kcal for men).
