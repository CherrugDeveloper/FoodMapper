## Nutritional bio hacking: what works and what is just marketing

The term *biohacking* refers to the deliberate use of interventions involving nutrition, sleep, exercise and supplementation to optimize physiological parameters. Not everything sold under this label is evidence-based. This article distinguishes practices with a scientific foundation from speculative strategies.

## Solid evidence

1. **Time-restricted eating**: condensing meals into an 8-12 hour window can improve insulin sensitivity and the metabolic circadian clock (Sutton et al., 2018). It is not magic: it works if it maintains an energy deficit.
2. **Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM)**: useful for diabetic or prediabetic subjects; for healthy people it mainly serves to learn individual responses to foods (Hall et al., 2018).
3. **Sleep as a metabolic lever**: 7-9 regular hours modulate ghrelin, leptin and cortisol; sleep deprivation reduces FODMAP tolerance through hyperactivity of the HPA axis (Kim et al., 2015).
4. **Targeted supplementation**: vitamin D, omega-3, magnesium and specific probiotics have evidence for target populations; a "universal stack" does not exist.

## Questionable strategies

- **Repeated prolonged fasting**: can induce metabolic adaptation and loss of lean mass if unsupervised.
- **Exogenous ketones**: do not accelerate fat loss if energy balance is neutral.
- **Extreme eliminations**: meat-only, fruit-only or liquid diets compromise microbiota diversity and can worsen IBS.

## Biohacking compatible with IBS

For people with irritable bowel, the only scientifically responsible biohacking is **increasing the resolution of subjective data**: food diary, sleep, symptoms and transit. This allows personal triggers to be isolated without falling into extreme protocols.
