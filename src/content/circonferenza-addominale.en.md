## What it measures

**Abdominal circumference**, often called waist circumference, is an indirect anthropometric measure of central adiposity. It can correlate with visceral fat and cardiometabolic risk, but it does not measure either directly. The word "abdominal" should not be confused with hip circumference or the maximum belly circumference when bloating is present.

## How to measure it reproducibly

1. Stand upright, with feet together or as specified by the protocol, abdomen relaxed and arms at the sides.
2. Use a non-stretch tape, horizontal and touching the skin without compressing it.
3. Identify and record the site: a common protocol uses the midpoint between the last palpable rib and iliac crest; others use the upper border of the iliac crest. Do not mix protocols when following changes.
4. Measure at the end of a normal breath out, without holding the breath or drawing the abdomen in.
5. Repeat at least once and record time, clothing, recent meal, menstrual cycle when relevant and bloating conditions.

For home monitoring, a measurement taken with the same technique is more useful than a seemingly precise but non-comparable one.

## Interpreting cut-offs

Cut-offs depend on organization, population, sex, age, ethnicity and measurement site. In harmonized metabolic-syndrome criteria, waist circumference is a **central-obesity** criterion with ethnicity-specific cut-offs; for European populations, **>=94 cm in men and >=80 cm in women** are often used, while higher values (>=102/88 cm) were used historically in some criteria. These are not individual diagnoses and should not be applied to pregnancy or every ethnic group without context.

A larger circumference is associated with higher risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease, but risk is continuous and does not suddenly begin beyond a number. Blood pressure, glucose, lipids, family history, medicines and physical activity complete the assessment.

## Distinguishing fat, bloating and transient changes

Meals, constipation, gas, fluid retention, the menstrual cycle and posture can temporarily change the value. A rapid change over a few days does not necessarily represent a change in visceral fat. New marked distension, persistent pain, vomiting, blood in stool, unintentional weight loss or breathing difficulty require medical advice rather than simple self-measurement.

## Limits

Circumference is influenced by skin, muscle, bowel contents, oedema and operator technique. It cannot by itself estimate body composition precisely, diagnose metabolic syndrome or determine treatment. Children, adolescents, pregnancy, athletes with substantial trunk muscle and people with conditions that alter abdominal shape require specific references.
